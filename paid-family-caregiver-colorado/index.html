In-Home Support Services

You are already the caregiver. You can also be the employee.

Colorado Medicaid can pay a family member for personal care and homemaker help they are already giving. As a licensed personal care and homemaker agency we do the employing, the payroll and the Medicaid billing — and we send someone else when you cannot be there.

What this is

The money is already there. It is the paperwork that is missing.

Colorado Medicaid pays for help at home, and has for years. What most families never learn is that the person delivering that help does not have to be a stranger from an agency roster. It can be the relative already doing it, funded through Community First Choice or through In-Home Support Services, usually shortened to IHSS — which of those applies is decided by the Case Management Agency, not by us.

There is one catch, and it is purely administrative: Medicaid will not send money to a private person. Somebody has to be the employer, run payroll and keep the records. That somebody is a licensed agency.

That is the whole of our part in it. Nothing about how you care for your relative changes.

Paying a relative is the route to look at

The next move is a conversation with the Case Management Agency for the county, who assess what help is needed at home, authorise the hours, and decide whether it runs through Community First Choice or In-Home Support Services. Once that exists, your family names an agency and names you as the caregiver. Ring us first if you would rather understand the whole path before you start it, and ask which route yours is: we are licensed for personal care and homemaker services.

You may be able to do both through one agency

With a waiver already in place, there is often more than one thing on the service plan — hours at home, respite, a day programme. You could be the paid caregiver for the hours at home while we cover the rest, which means one intake and one set of people who know your relative. Worth talking through with us and with the case manager together.

You are in a good position to start

A waiver and a case manager are the hard part, and they are already done. What happens next is that your family chooses who delivers each service on the plan. Tell your case manager you would like Passionate Care considered, or call us and we will explain what we would need from them.

The Community Centered Board is where this starts

Respite, day programmes, transport and residential support run on disability waivers, and a Community Centered Board decides who is eligible and coordinates the plan. There can be a wait, and it is a state process rather than an agency one. We can tell you which CCB covers your address and what to ask for.

Agency-directed home care is probably the fit

Where a family does not want to employ anyone or manage a schedule, we send our own caregivers and take care of the rest. A Case Management Agency assesses what is needed and authorises the hours first. Community First Choice is often the funding behind it, and it has no waiver waiting list attached.

Your in-home hours may already be on the plan

People on a disability waiver often have personal care or homemaker hours in their service plan alongside everything else, and in many cases those hours can move to Community First Choice instead. Ask the case manager how the plan is currently built — and we are happy to be on that call.

Health First Colorado enrolment comes first

Every one of these services is paid for through Colorado Medicaid, so enrolment is the doorway to all of them. Plenty of families start exactly here, and it is not a dead end. We can point you at the right office and tell you what usually follows.

We may not reach you yet

Write to us and we will give you a straight answer about whether we expect to reach you, and suggest where else to look if we do not.

How the work divides

You keep the caring. We take the administration.

Families worry that becoming an employee will mean losing control of how their relative is looked after. It works the other way round. Under IHSS the household holds the decisions and the agency holds the bureaucracy.

The path

Four steps, and the first two are not ours

Timelines vary, and the parts that take longest are the parts nobody at an agency controls.

  1. Health First Colorado

    The person receiving care needs to be enrolled in Colorado Medicaid. If they already are, skip straight past this.

  2. An assessment

    A Case Management Agency meets them, works out what help they need at home, and authorises a number of hours.

  3. Pick the agency and the caregiver

    Your family names Passionate Care, and names the relative who will be paid. That is usually the person already doing it.

  4. Hiring, then payroll

    We onboard the caregiver, complete the required training and the required checks, and start paying. The caring never paused.

Nobody here can promise eligibility, a number of hours, or a rate of pay. Those are set by the Case Management Agency and by Colorado Medicaid rules. What we can promise is a straight answer about whether this looks likely for your household, including when the answer is no.

One thing worth asking early: we are licensed for personal care and homemaker services, which is what Community First Choice funds. If the Case Management Agency routes your household through In-Home Support Services instead, ask us before you plan around it and we will tell you plainly where we stand.

If a disability waiver is involved

Being paid at home does not have to be the only thing

Where the person you care for has a developmental disability waiver, their service plan often carries more than in-home hours. There may be respite so you get a genuine break, a day programme that gets them out among other people, and transport to get them there.

Because we are approved for those services as well, one family can have all of it from one agency. Tell us what your week looks like and we will say what is possible.

Two acronyms, once

  • IHSS — In-Home Support Services. The person receiving care picks and directs their own caregiver; the agency employs them.
  • CFC — Community First Choice. A Colorado Medicaid state plan benefit covering attendant help at home, with no waiver waiting list.
Questions families ask

The things people ring up about

Will Colorado Medicaid pay me to look after my son?

In many cases it will. If your son is on Health First Colorado and an assessment finds he needs regular help at home, In-Home Support Services lets him choose who provides that help — and a parent of an adult son can be that person. He directs the work; we employ you and bill Medicaid. Whether he is authorised for these services — and for how long each week — is a decision his Case Management Agency makes, and we have no part in it.

Which relatives are allowed to be paid?

Adult children, parents of an adult son or daughter, brothers and sisters, grandparents, grandchildren, aunts, uncles, cousins and in-laws can commonly be paid. Two situations carry extra rules: a spouse, and a parent of a child under eighteen. Neither is automatically ruled out, but both depend on the specific programme and the current state rules. Ring us if that is your household; we will share what we know and name who actually decides.

Do I need a certificate or previous experience?

No. The whole idea behind In-Home Support Services is that the person receiving care trains the person giving it, because they know how they like things done. There is no nursing assistant certificate to earn first. You complete our onboarding and the training the state requires. Health maintenance activities are a separate matter: each one has to be signed off by a registered nurse before anybody takes it on, so ask about those specifically.

What will I actually be paid?

The rate is not ours to set, so any figure from us would be a guess. Pay follows the service that was authorised, the hours approved, and Colorado Medicaid rules. We can walk you through how a rate gets arrived at, and what a particular authorisation would mean week to week once you are holding it.

Can I keep my other job?

Yes, and a lot of people do. The schedule belongs to the household rather than to us, so hours can be arranged around a shift pattern or a school run — inside whatever the assessment has authorised.

My relative has a disability waiver. Does that change anything?

It can make things simpler, because we can deliver both sides. Someone on a developmental disability waiver may have in-home hours, respite, a day programme and transport in the same service plan. You could be the paid caregiver for the hours at home while we cover the respite and the day programme — without a second agency, a second intake, or your relative telling their story twice.

My relative has no Medicaid at all. Is that the end of it?

Then that is the first step, and it is an ordinary place to begin rather than a dead end. Health First Colorado enrolment comes first, then the assessment that decides what in-home help is authorised. We can point you at the right office. We cannot determine eligibility ourselves, and no agency can.
