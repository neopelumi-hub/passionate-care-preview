Community First Choice · Colorado

Help at home without joining a waiting list

Community First Choice is a Colorado Medicaid benefit for attendant support at home. It sits in the state plan rather than in a waiver — which is why there is no queue for a place on it.

Ask how this applies to you Call (720) 000-0000
The distinction that matters

A waiver has a queue. The state plan does not.

Colorado pays for long-term support in two different ways, and the difference is not academic — it decides how long a family waits.

A waiver is a programme with a limited number of places. It can fund things the ordinary Medicaid package does not, but places are finite and there can be a wait.

A state plan benefit is part of the standard package. Community First Choice is one of these. Someone enrolled in Health First Colorado who meets the criteria can receive it without waiting for a place to open up.

Worth asking the case manager

  • How are the in-home hours on this service plan currently funded?
  • Could any of them be funded through Community First Choice instead?
  • What would change on the plan if they were?
  • Would that free anything up elsewhere?

Those are the questions. The answers belong to the case manager.

A situation we see

The hours were already there. They were just in the wrong column.

Picture a young woman in Thornton on a developmental disability waiver. Her service plan has a day programme, some respite for her father, and a handful of personal care hours for mornings. The family has assumed for years that every one of those things is rationed by the same waiver.

At a plan review, the case manager raises the possibility of the morning hours being funded through Community First Choice instead. Nothing about her mornings changes. What changes is which column pays for them — and that is the sort of thing that only surfaces if somebody asks.

We are not the ones who can make that call. What we can do is deliver the hours once they are authorised, under either funding route and under either arrangement, because we hold the home care licence that allows it.

What it pays for

Attendant support, in three kinds

The same three service types that run under In-Home Support Services. What an individual is authorised for comes from their assessment, not from this list.

Personal care

Washing, dressing, grooming, using the toilet, eating, and moving safely between a bed, a chair and the front door.

Homemaker services

Keeping the household going when a disability makes it hard: cleaning, laundry, cooking, the shopping, the errands nobody else has time for.

Health maintenance activities

Nurse-level tasks a caregiver may take on after a registered nurse has watched them do it and signed them off — medication, tube feeding, catheter care, a bowel programme, blood-sugar checks.

Who this reaches

Not only people with a disability waiver

Because it is a state plan benefit, Community First Choice is not confined to the developmental disability world. Older adults, people with physical disabilities, people living with a brain injury, and people with complex health needs may all be assessed as needing attendant support at home. Three acronyms, once

  • CFC — Community First Choice. The state plan benefit described on this page.
  • IHSS — In-Home Support Services. The member-directed way of arranging the work.
  • HCBS — Home and Community Based Services. The umbrella term for support delivered where people live rather than in an institution.
CFC questions

The things people get wrong about this one

What is Community First Choice?

It is part of Colorado’s standard Medicaid package, and it funds attendant support at home: help with everyday personal tasks, keeping a household running, and certain health maintenance activities. It is usually shortened to CFC. The important thing about it is that it sits in the state plan rather than in a waiver, which changes how people get to it.

What does "state plan benefit" mean, in plain words?

A waiver is a limited programme with a fixed number of places, which is why waiting lists exist. A state plan benefit is part of the ordinary Medicaid package: if someone is enrolled and meets the criteria, it is available to them without queueing for a place. That is the single biggest practical difference between Community First Choice and waiver-funded services.

We already have a disability waiver. Is CFC relevant to us?

Very often, yes, and it is one of the most useful things a family can ask about. Where someone on a developmental disability waiver has personal care or homemaker hours in their service plan, those hours may be able to be funded through Community First Choice instead. Ask the case manager directly how the plan is currently built and whether moving those hours is possible for that person. We are happy to be on the call.

Does using CFC mean losing waiver services?

No. It is not a swap of one for the other. Community First Choice covers attendant support at home; things like respite, day habilitation, transport and residential support are waiver services and stay where they are. How the two fit together for a particular person is set out in their service plan, which the case manager holds.

Can a family member be paid under Community First Choice?

Where the hours are arranged member-directed, the person receiving care chooses their own attendant, and that can be a relative. The rules about which relatives, and about spouses and parents of minors, are the same questions that arise under In-Home Support Services, and they are settled by the Case Management Agency for the specific household.

Who decides whether someone qualifies?

A Case Management Agency, following an assessment. Not an agency like ours, and not a website. If someone is not yet enrolled in Health First Colorado, enrolment is the first step, because every part of this runs through it.
