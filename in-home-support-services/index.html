In-Home Support Services, explained without the jargon

IHSS is not a benefit you apply for. It is a way of arranging help at home in which your household holds the decisions and an agency holds the paperwork.

Be the paid caregiver Call (720) 000-0000
Three roles

Everything about IHSS follows from who holds which job

Families get tangled in this because the word “support” sounds like a service and it is really a structure.

The person receiving care

Chooses the caregiver. Sets the schedule. Says how things should be done. Supervises the work. If they cannot do that themselves, an authorised representative does it on their behalf — usually a parent or another close relative.

The caregiver

Does the work, on the household’s terms. Very often a relative who has been doing it unpaid. Becomes an employee of the agency, with a payslip, tax withheld and workers’ compensation cover.

The agency

Employs the caregiver, runs payroll, bills Medicaid, keeps the records, arranges the oversight the programme requires, and finds cover when the caregiver cannot work. Does not choose who the caregiver is.

The real choice

Who runs the arrangement — you, or us?

This is the decision that actually matters, and most families are never offered it plainly. Both columns below are ordinary, both are funded the same way, and a family can start in one and move to the other.

Member-directed — In-Home Support Services

You run it

  • You name the caregiver, including a relative
  • You set and change the schedule
  • You train them in how things are done here
  • You supervise, and you raise it with us when something is wrong
  • We employ, pay, bill and provide backup

Suits families who already have someone in mind, or who want the same face every day.

Agency-directed

We run it

  • We match a caregiver from our own team
  • We build the rota and cover the gaps
  • We train and supervise them
  • You tell us what is working and what is not
  • We employ, pay, bill and oversee, as before

Suits households with nobody available to take it on, or who simply do not want to manage staff.

Whichever column fits, the funding behind it is the same Colorado Medicaid money, and the eligibility decision is made in the same place. Read about Community First Choice, which is the benefit that most often pays for these hours.

If someone cannot direct their own care

The authorised representative is the part nobody explains

A programme built on the person choosing their own caregiver raises an obvious question for families supporting someone with a significant intellectual or developmental disability, or dementia, or a brain injury. Somebody else can hold the directing role on their behalf.

In practice that is usually a parent who has been making these calls for twenty years already. It does not push the person out of their own life — they stay in the decisions as far as they are able to be. It simply means the paperwork has somebody to sign it.

Who may act as an authorised representative, and how one is appointed, is settled with the Case Management Agency. Ask us and we will tell you who to call.

What can be delivered under IHSS

  • Personal care — bathing, dressing, grooming, the bathroom, help with eating, transfers, moving about
  • Homemaker — cleaning, laundry, meals, shopping, errands
  • Health maintenance activities — nurse-level tasks after a registered nurse has validated the caregiver’s skill at that task

What each of these looks like day to day

How it is set up

The order things happen in

  1. Assessment and authorisation

    A Case Management Agency establishes what help is needed at home and authorises hours. This is also where member-directed versus agency-directed is decided and recorded.

  2. Naming the agency and the caregiver

    The person, or their authorised representative, names us as the agency and names the caregiver. There is no roster to be assigned from.

  3. Hiring, training, oversight

    We onboard the caregiver properly — employment paperwork, required training and the required checks. Health maintenance activities need a registered nurse to sign off each task first, and are arranged separately.

Nothing on this page is a promise of eligibility, hours or pay. Those are determined by the Case Management Agency under Colorado Medicaid rules. See what this looks like for a family who wants to be paid.

IHSS questions

What people ask once they have heard the term

What does In-Home Support Services actually mean?

It is a way of arranging Medicaid-funded help at home in which the person receiving the care is in charge of it. They choose who provides it, set the hours, decide how tasks are done, and supervise the work day to day. A licensed agency sits behind them as the legal employer, handling payroll, Medicaid billing and the employment records. IHSS is the arrangement, not a separate pot of money.

How is IHSS different from ordinary home care?

In ordinary agency-directed care, the agency picks the caregiver, builds the schedule, trains the worker and supervises them. Under IHSS all four of those move to the household. The trade is control for responsibility: you gain the say over who comes into the house and when, and you take on the job of directing them. Neither is better. They suit different families, and a family can change its mind later.

What if the person cannot direct their own care?

Then an authorised representative directs it for them. This matters enormously for families supporting someone with an intellectual or developmental disability, or with dementia, or a brain injury. A parent, sibling or other trusted person takes on the directing role, and the person receiving care stays at the centre of the decisions as far as they are able to be. Who can serve as an authorised representative, and how one is appointed, is confirmed through the Case Management Agency.

Which services can be delivered this way?

Personal care, homemaker services, and health maintenance activities. Personal care is hands-on help with the body. Homemaker is help keeping the household running. Health maintenance activities are nurse-level tasks a caregiver may take on after a registered nurse has watched them do it and signed them off for that one task. What a particular person is authorised for comes from their assessment.

Can a family member really be the paid caregiver?

Yes, and it is one of the main reasons the arrangement exists. Adult children, parents of adult sons and daughters, siblings, grandparents, grandchildren and in-laws are commonly paid this way. Spouses and parents of a child under eighteen carry extra rules and are not automatically ruled out. That is a question for the Case Management Agency about the specific household.

Who decides how many hours we get?

The Case Management Agency, after an assessment of what help the person needs at home. It is not something an agency can influence, add to, or promise. If the hours on an authorisation do not match what a week actually demands, the conversation to have is with the case manager, and we can help you describe the week accurately.
