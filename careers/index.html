Three ways in

The roles, and who each one suits

All three are real jobs with a payslip, tax withheld and workers’ compensation cover.

Caregiver, in someone’s home

Personal care and homemaking. Steady hours with the same person or two. Suits people who would rather go deep with a few households than shallow with twenty.

Direct Support Professional

Respite and the day programme. Out in the community far more than in a building — volunteering shifts, classes, the gym, the bus. Suits people with energy, patience and a genuine appetite for other people’s company.

Family member being paid

If you already care for a relative, you may be able to be employed for it. You keep doing what you do; we handle the employment and the billing. How that works.

What we look for

Reliability beats a CV, most days

There is no certificate to earn before you start. The training the state requires happens after you are hired, not before.

What we cannot train into somebody is turning up. A person with an intellectual or developmental disability who has been let down by a rota does not experience that as an administrative issue. It is the single thing we screen hardest for.

What the job asks

  • Turning up, reliably, when you said you would
  • Patience with people who communicate differently
  • Willingness to complete required training and checks
  • Treating a household as somebody’s home, not a workplace
  • Saying when something is wrong instead of hoping

What you get

  • Employment with tax withheld and workers’ compensation cover
  • Training, and the checks the state requires
  • Consistent placements rather than a different address daily
  • Someone to ring who knows the people you support

Pay follows the authorised service and Colorado Medicaid rules; we will discuss it plainly with you and will not quote a figure on a web page.

Getting started

How to put your name forward

  1. Tell us you are interested

    Use the contact form or ring. Name, county, and a line about what kind of work you are after. Please do not send medical or personal detail through the form.

  2. A conversation

    Not a formal interview to begin with. We want to know what you are good at, what you would not want to do, and what hours actually work for your life.

  3. Checks, training, and a match

    The required background checks and training, then a placement chosen to fit rather than whichever gap is open. Matching is the whole job in this work.

Family members: if you are here because you already care for a relative, the route is a little different — it starts with their Case Management Agency rather than with us. Start on the paid family caregiver page, or ring and we will walk you through it.

Interested? Say hello

You do not need experience or a certificate to start the conversation. Tell us which county you are in and what sort of hours would work.
